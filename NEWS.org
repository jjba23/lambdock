*[Released]*

** v0.19.1 (2026-10-29)
- Hovering the dock no longer resizes it, which is what made it flicker and
  tear along its edges. The fish-eye grows the icons, and growing widgets inside
  a window makes GTK resize the window: on Wayland that is a buffer
  re-allocation and a compositor reconfigure on *every* pointer frame. The room
  the row can spread into is now reserved once, before the pointer starts
  magnifying, so the surface keeps its size for the whole hover; a dock that was
  sweeping the pointer across a long row was reconfiguring its surface dozens of
  times a second, and now it does it once when the pointer arrives.
- The room the dock reserves -- including the headroom above the capsule that was
  always there -- is no longer part of what it listens to. The surface's input
  region is cut down to the capsule plus the row of icons (the bounds are recomputed
  when the dock is mapped, when its geometry changes, and while it magnifies), so
  the invisible band around the dock cannot swallow a click meant for the window
  underneath. Before this, the transparent area above the dock was a dead zone.
- The icon-zoom test no longer snapshots a widget between a resize and its
  allocation, which is a GTK warning and a flaky test on a loaded machine.

*[Released]*

** v0.19.0 (2026-10-29)
- The clock item is an analogue face instead of a time string. It is drawn from
  geometry -- no artwork at any size -- as a squircle body with a brushed bezel,
  a paper dial shaded from the top-left, twelve hour ticks and sixty minute
  ticks, an hour, minute and second hand, and one sweep of glass across the body:
  a watch face is an object, so it keeps its own colours rather than following
  the theme, and it takes exactly one icon cell instead of pushing a string's
  worth of width into the row. The poll function keeps its job -- the string it
  returns is the face's tooltip and what a screen reader announces -- while the
  hands are read from the system clock, so the dock shows the right time even
  when its own timer was skipped while it was hidden.
- The face's geometry (where each hand points, and where a squircle runs) is a
  pure function with its own test, because an hour hand on the wrong minute is
  the kind of bug nobody notices until it is three o'clock.
- The showcase recording and its stills are re-recorded, and the recorder's item
  coordinates are re-measured: a face is a cell narrower than the string it
  replaced, and the dock is centred, so every item before it moved.

*[Released]*

** v0.18.2 (2026-10-29)
- Dynamic items line up with the icons next to them. Three things were off, and
  all three came from sizing the wrong widget:
  - A gauge, a separator and the clock were shorter than an icon, and the row
    anchors items to the dock edge, so they hung lower than the icons they sit
    between. The icon-sized cell -- not the button -- now carries the cross
    size, and every item's content sits in one, so the theme's button padding
    is outside the sum instead of missing from it.
  - On a shelf theme (the two macOS Mavericks materials) the gauges sat a whole
    reflection band lower than the icons: a meter dropped its reflection widget
    when it was built, and that widget is what reserves the band at the bottom
    of each item. Every item now gets a reflection slot, empty when it has
    nothing to mirror, and a gauge clears the artwork but keeps the slot.
  - The separator lost its height when it moved into a cell (a =GtkSeparator=
    is one pixel by default); it fills its cell now, so it spans the icons'
    height and is centred on them.
- The showcase recording and its stills are re-recorded with the fix, so the
  Mavericks shelf frames show the gauges level with the icons.

*[Released]*

** v0.18.1 (2026-10-29)
- The two macOS Mavericks materials are in the showcase, and shown as the shelf
  they are written for.  =macos-mavericks.css= and =macos-mavericks-dark.css=
  are the *material* only: the polished platinum slab with the icons mirrored
  onto the glass in front of them only appears once the dock takes the matching
  shelf geometry (=dock-shelf-margin -22=, =dock-shelf-front 18=,
  =dock-reflection 0.5=), which is exactly the trio the stylesheet documents.
  The montage ends its dark half on macOS Mavericks Dark and its light half on
  macOS Mavericks, each held for a beat longer than a straight colour swap (the
  slab slides in, and the mirrors have to be readable), and the light one stays
  on screen after the last swap instead of losing its label in the same frame.
  The REPL helper the scene teaches now takes the shelf as a second argument --
  ~(swap! 'macos-mavericks 'shelf)~ -- because a theme and its geometry have to
  travel in one expression: ~reload-dock!~ only queues a redraw, so a geometry
  change issued between two reloads lands in the same redraw as the neighbouring
  theme and paints the wrong pair.
- The Preferences scene of the recording shows the dialog on its own.  Two
  things were wrong: the window popover's *Close All Windows* row was being
  clicked twenty pixels into the separator below it (the row coordinates had been
  measured against an older layout, and the rows are anchored from the bottom so
  the number of windows moves them), so the demo's terminals never closed and
  then sat behind the dialog; and the dialog is a tiled window unless it is
  explicitly floated, so it opened full width with the floating terminals on top
  of it.  The rows are re-measured, the scene clears the stage itself, the
  recorder's niri config opens the dialog floating at the size the scene uses
  (instead of tiling it full width and resizing it on camera), and the dialog is
  floated, sized and focused before it is held -- low enough that the caption
  pill no longer sits on its title bar.
- The taskbar scene is captioned for what it shows.  It said "with window
  titles", but the label taskbar mode adds for a running window is measured away
  in this layout -- the button is allocated less than its content asks for and
  the title ellipsizes to "..." -- so the scene now says "Or a Windows XP-style
  taskbar" and the recording no longer promises a detail that is not on screen.
  The toggle itself is unchanged and still in Preferences.

** v0.18.0 (2026-10-29)
- The showcase recording is now a two-stage pipeline -- =record-demo.sh= records,
  =encode-demo.sh= frames and encodes -- and the GIF it produces is presented
  instead of merely captured: the screen sits inset in a dark backdrop with a
  rounded, shadowed edge, captions arrived as rounded pills at the top of the
  screen, theme names get their own label, and the intro and outro are dimmed
  title cards.  The whole presentation layer (=tools/record-demo/make_overlay.py=)
  works in output coordinates, so it does not depend on the compositor's layout.
  A 72-second version of the previous storyboard was 15 MiB; the new ~60-second
  one fits the same budget with more of the frame in use.
- The pointer that drives the demo now belongs to the *host* compositor.  A
  virtual pointer inside the nested session delivers clicks but does not move
  pointer focus, so a hidden dock never saw the pointer reach its reveal strip
  and the fish-eye never started -- two headline features could not be filmed at
  all.  Driving the host pointer makes the nested compositor see an ordinary
  pointer crossing its window, and because a virtual pointer does not move the
  host's hardware cursor, the recording contains exactly one cursor: the real one
  the compositor draws.  The cursor the old encoder painted in post (which
  drifted out of sync because its ~\move~ used absolute timestamps) is gone.
- The storyboard was rewritten rather than retimed: twelve scenes, each about
  four seconds, each one checked frame by frame against its caption.  The window
  popover, the dock's own menu and the Preferences window are now actually shown
  (their click targets were calibrated against an older layout and had been
  missing their marks, so one scene opened the wrong menu and removed a dock
  item).  The demo terminals run a generated demo shell and show a system
  summary and a file listing instead of an empty prompt, and closing the REPL
  terminal no longer leaves kitty's "are you sure?" dialog on screen for the
  last ten seconds.
- Recording and event time are now in sync.  The recorder used to wait for a log
  line current =wf-recorder= builds never print, so it always waited its full
  timeout and started capturing ten seconds early: every caption landed ten
  seconds before the thing it described.  It now waits for the capture file to
  grow, and the encoder trims the tail at the last event, so a slow recorder
  shutdown cannot add dead air either.
- The capture is cropped only at the top, at 1440 px, and downsampled once by
  the encoder: nothing of the dock is cut (a taskbar spans the whole width) and
  text stays crisp after the palette pass.  The generated wallpapers
  were rebuilt as low-frequency gradients -- the old jagged mountain ridges and
  dense star field aliased into visible moire once the GIF was downscaled -- and
  the light one is now a soft haze instead of hard-edged polygons.
- The recorder no longer draws its own cursor and no longer needs a checklist of
  coordinates in the docs: =tools/record-demo/measure_dock.py= measures the dock's
  capsule and item centres in a recording and prints them ready to paste.  That
  is how the storyboard's table was rebuilt, and the tool is the answer to
  "adding an item moved everything".
- Fixing the run also exposed a real dock bug: while an icon is magnified the
  capsule keeps its resting width, so the row overflows and the clock is clipped
  mid-glyph.  It is visible in every hover frame of the old GIF.  (Tracked, not
  fixed here.)
- The launchpad search field takes the keyboard the moment the launchpad opens:
  pressing a key filters the grid without clicking the field first.  Two bugs
  behind that: the field was never remembered on the launchpad model, and the
  grid's column fitting was connected to GTK3's ~size-allocate~ signal, which no
  longer exists in GTK4 (the grid now fits itself when the surface maps).
- Launchpad tiles are larger by default (~dock-launchpad-icon-size~ is 80 instead
  of 64), which is the one setting people changed first.
- Minimize to dock: clicking the icon of an application whose single window
  already has the focus now minimizes it, and clicking it again restores it.  The
  dock tracks the ~minimized~/~activated~ state the compositor reports and asks
  for ~set_minimized~ through the ~wlr-foreign-toplevel~ protocol, so it depends
  on the compositor having a minimize concept at all (scrollable tiling
  compositors such as niri do not, and simply ignore the request).  Applications
  with several windows still open the window popover.  The icon dims while all of
  its windows are minimized.
- A quality pass over the running dock, across compositors.  On a compositor
  without ~wlr-layer-shell~ (GNOME, or an X11 session) ~lambdock~ now says so once
  and runs as an ordinary GTK window instead of logging a critical per layer-shell
  call; the same run also stopped claiming to be "hidden" while it is still on
  screen, which used to freeze the hover magnification after the first auto-hide.
  On compositors without ~wlr-foreign-toplevel~ the missing running-app support is
  reported once, and a missing ~wl_seat~ no longer makes activation fail silently.
  A window that closed while its popover was open could leave the menu holding a
  freed Wayland handle; it no longer can.
- Performance: the fisheye now steps its *layout* by 2 px while keeping the painted
  magnification continuous, which halves the dock row's re-measures during a hover
  sweep (23 -> 12 distinct sizes for a full sweep, measured in the test suite).
  Dynamic-item and trash polls stop firing while the dock is hidden and refresh
  once when it comes back, the animation-enabled setting is cached instead of read
  per frame, and identical window titles no longer re-push labels and accessible
  text.  Reflections draw at most 16 alpha bands instead of 40.
- Polish: the launchpad fades in when it opens (and simply appears when motion is
  reduced), and every shipped theme was checked against GTK's CSS parser at
  runtime -- all of them parse clean.
- The Windows XP theme was rebuilt against Luna reference material: a flat blue
  slab with a single bright top line instead of a glossy mirrored gradient, square
  task buttons with one 1px blue border instead of a white outline on every icon,
  pinned launchers with no chrome at all (like Quick Launch), Tahoma-sized 11px
  labels, and the focused application drawn as the depressed task button. Hover
  and press now ease their border and shadow over 120ms. Two new state classes
  back this -- ~window-focused~ and ~window-minimized~ -- and every theme can use
  them.
- The showcase GIF is generated from this tree and lives in the repository
  (=resources/img/lambdock-demo.gif=), as the hero of the README.  Per-feature
  stills from the same recording are in =resources/img/screens/=, and the
  AppStream metadata points at them.
- The launchpad is scriptable: the REPL gained ~(show-launchpad!)~,
  ~(launchpad-search! "...")~ and ~(hide-launchpad!)~, which is how the demo
  films its search without a virtual keyboard.
- Fixed a crash that a REPL-driven reload could trigger while reflections were
  being enabled: the Guile bridge validated only some of the item accessors it
  then applied, and the dynamic-item refresh could apply ~#f~ as a poll
  procedure.  Both are now checked, so a module that lost an export degrades to
  "no items" instead of aborting the process.

** v0.15.0 (2026-10-28)
- Stacks no longer offer the *fan* view.  Two views remain, the icon grid and
  the list; a settings file or override that still says `'fan` (or anything
  else unknown) reads back as the grid, so nothing breaks and nothing throws.
  The context menu of a stack now shows one switch button fewer.
- The project moved from the GNU General Public License v3.0-or-later to the
  MIT License. Every source file now starts with a three line SPDX header
  (=SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT=), =COPYING= carries the MIT text, and the
  license every package metadata declares -- the Arch, Debian, openSUSE and
  Flatpak packaging, the Nix, Guix and maak manifests, the AppStream metadata,
  the container labels and the About dialog -- says MIT as well.
- The launchpad keeps its ten pieces of file-scope state in one model struct,
  and its search filter and column arithmetic became pure functions with their
  own tests (they no longer need a display to be exercised). No behaviour change.
- A pass over the C side for state and safety.  The active docks now live in one
  registry that hands out snapshots instead of the list itself; the dimension
  arithmetic, the configuration clamping and the name-to-value rules moved into
  pure, display-free modules with their own tests; a dock item kind, a Scheme
  item type and a stack view are each one row of a table instead of a switch;
  and the Guile bridge no longer lets Guile's `malloc`'d strings escape, so every
  free site is a `g_free`.  Nothing user-visible changes, apart from fewer ways
  for a hostile settings file to break the layout.

** v0.13.3 (2026-10-27)
- The launchpad no longer shows one application twice. Two desktop files that
  describe the same program -- `libreoffice-writer.desktop` and
  `org.libreoffice.LibreOffice.writer.desktop` for the distro package and the
  Flatpak export of it -- each used to get a tile. Entries are now collapsed
  when they share a name and the window class they declare, or the icon when
  neither declares a class, keeping the entry the scan sees first (your own
  directory, then Flatpak, then the system). Same-named applications that really
  are two applications, like two panels called *Displays*, are still both
  listed. The copy that does not get the tile is not lost: the tile's context
  menu (right click, *Menu* or *Shift+F10*) lists every installed copy with
  where it comes from (Flatpak, your own files, the system) and the command it
  runs, so the copy that starts is the one the user picks. A tile whose
  application is installed once has no menu, and the cache of window-to-
  application lookups still indexes the copies that lost, so a window of a
  distro package keeps resolving to the application of its Flatpak sibling.
- The launchpad backdrop is denser, so the labels stay legible over a busy or
  bright desktop: the solid background goes from 78% to 94% opacity and the
  frosted one from 50% to 82%. A real blur of what is behind it is still the
  compositor's job, as documented in *The launchpad*.

*[Released]*

** v0.13.2 (2026-10-26)
- The right-click menu now carries the actions that belong to the kind of
  application the item is. Browsers get *New Window* and *New Private Window*
  (Firefox's `--private-window`, Chrome/Chromium/Brave/Vivaldi's `--incognito`,
  Edge's `--inprivate`, Opera's `--private`), and a running music player gets
  *Play/Pause*, *Previous Track*, *Next Track* and *Stop Playback* over MPRIS.
  Applications are classified from their desktop entry's `Categories=`
  (`WebBrowser`, `Music`, `Audio;Player`), with their id, name and command line
  as the fallback, so a hand written Scheme item for Spotify is understood as
  well as one that came from a desktop file. Playback controls only appear while
  the player is actually on the session bus, so a pinned but closed player does
  not grow buttons that would do nothing.

** v0.13.1 (2026-10-26)
- The container image builds again. The Dockerfile copies the sources it needs
  one directory at a time and `data/` was never among them, so `meson setup` in
  the build stage failed with "File data/org.jointhefreeworld.lambdock.desktop
  does not exist" from the moment the desktop entry and the AppStream metadata
  became part of the build -- which is why Docker Hub had been sitting at
  v0.7.4. With `data/` copied, the image builds, runs its test suite and
  installs the same desktop entry and AppStream data as an ordinary build.

** v0.13.0 (2026-10-26)
- Accessibility and keyboard pass. The dock can be driven without a pointer:
  Left/Right (Up/Down on a side dock) walk the icons, Home/End jump to the ends,
  Enter/Space activate, Menu or Shift+F10 opens the context menu, Escape closes
  whatever is open, and Ctrl+Arrow rearranges the icons (and stores the new order
  like a drag does). Stack grids, lists and the fan take the arrow keys too, the
  launchpad grid moves by tile with Down/Enter from the search field, and the
  preferences window closes with Escape. The dock's layer surface now asks the
  compositor for the keyboard on demand, without which none of this was
  reachable.
- Screen reader support: every dock icon has an accessible name and description
  that follow its state ("Terminal, Running, 2 windows", "Projects, folder
  stack", "Trash, 3 items in the trash"), as do launchpad tiles, stack entries
  and preference controls. The stack fan announces the selected card and its
  position, and keyboard focus is drawn with a visible ring on the dock, the
  launchpad, the stacks and the preferences window.
- Reduced motion: the new `dock-reduce-motion?` setting (Preferences →
  Accessibility) stops the dock's slide and fish-eye animations; the desktop's
  own `gtk-enable-animations` accessibility setting is honoured too, and changes
  there take effect on the running dock immediately.
- Left-clicking the trash now reliably lands in the file manager's trash view.
  The previous code only tried a handler for the `trash:///` scheme, which most
  distributions do not register; it now also hands the URI to the application
  that handles folders (the user's file manager), and only falls back to the
  home trash directory when neither can open it.
- Rework the stack fan. The cards form a real arc with the centre card upright,
  the hovered card comes to the front with its full name printed above the fan,
  the card and text colours follow the theme instead of assuming a light
  background, and a wide fan scrolls sideways instead of collapsing into
  unreadable slivers. Image files now show a live thumbnail of their contents in
  every view -- fan cards, grid tiles and list rows -- centre-cropped to a
  square; other files keep their file-type icon. Thumbnails decode on a worker
  thread, one at a time, so opening a folder of large photos neither stalls the
  dock nor holds a dozen decoded images in memory, and closing the popover stops
  the remaining decodes. The stack grid and list views are larger too: bigger
  icons, two-line names and roomier rows.
- Clicking an entry in a stack popover now dismisses the popover once the entry
  has been opened, instead of leaving the panel over the file manager or
  application it just launched.
- Escape now closes stack popovers even when focus is still on the dock icon,
  not only when a popover child has the keyboard focus.
- The launchpad gets a close button in the top-right corner, Escape works while
  the search field has focus, and it can be configured from `settings.scm` or
  the preferences window: `dock-launchpad-icon-size`, `dock-launchpad-columns`
  and `dock-launchpad-blur?` for a frosted, translucent backdrop (blurred by
  compositors that blur layer surfaces; a scoped layer rule is documented in the
  README).
- The preferences window is easier to read: each group is a rounded card with a
  short explanation, spacing between sections is wider, and every numeric row
  has a spin button beside its slider so an exact value (a 53px icon, a 0.07
  blur) can be typed instead of approximated by dragging.

** v0.12.1 (2026-10-26)
- The trash item accepts files and folders dropped on it. The drop calls GIO's
  `g_file_trash()`, so the file lands in the trash of its own filesystem, a name
  that is already taken gets a fresh one instead of overwriting, and folders go
  with everything inside them. It runs off the main loop like emptying does, and
  the icon switches to its full artwork as soon as the files are in. The drop
  target belongs to the trash item itself, so a file dropped there never becomes
  a stack or an application in the dock.
- Cover it in tests: `tests/test_trash.c` trashes files, folders and colliding
  names into a throwaway trash (with `HOME` and `XDG_DATA_HOME` redirected so it
  never touches the real one), and `test_dock_item` checks that the trash item
  carries a file-list drop target.

** v0.12.0 (2026-10-26)
- Add a trash item. `(trash-item)` puts the desktop trash on the dock: the icon
  follows it (the theme's `user-trash` while empty, `user-trash-full` once it
  holds anything) and the tooltip says how many items it holds. Left click opens
  the trash in the file manager; the context menu shows the count with *Open
  Trash* and *Empty Trash*, the latter disabled when there is nothing to empty.
- Read and write the trash the way the freedesktop.org Trash specification
  defines it: the home trash in `$XDG_DATA_HOME/Trash`, plus every mounted
  volume's `$topdir/.Trash/$uid` or `$topdir/.Trash-$uid`. No `gio`, GVfs or
  GNOME/KDE helper is involved, so the item behaves the same on GNOME, KDE,
  Xfce, Cinnamon, MATE and the rest, and a deletion made in any file manager
  shows up within two seconds. Emptying removes orphaned files and stale
  metadata too, copes with trashed directories that kept read-only modes, and
  runs off the main loop so a large trash does not freeze the dock.
- Cover it in tests: `tests/test_trash.c` for the layout, counting and emptying
  (in throwaway trash directories only), plus the trash item in the dock-item,
  popover-menu and Guile-bridge suites, and `trash-item` in the Scheme suite.

** v0.10.9 (2026-10-26)
- Remove the preferences test's temporary directory when it finishes. Every

  Leftover directories also confused Guix's `install-license-files` phase,

** v0.10.8 (2026-10-26)
- Add a GTK4 preferences window. Right-click the dock and choose *Preferences…*
  to change auto-hide, hover magnification, window tracking, dock/taskbar mode,
  icon size, spacing, capsule padding, screen edge, theme and every glass-shelf
  knob from the GUI. Each control applies live through the same `set-dock-*!`
  setters the REPL uses, and the values are remembered in
  `$XDG_STATE_HOME/lambdock/preferences-<settings>.scm` -- a separate override
  file that the loader layers over `settings.scm`, so regenerating the settings
  file does not discard GUI choices. *Reset* (or deleting that file) restores
  the settings file.
- Ship `xz` in the Guix test manifest. gtk4's pkg-config chain (gdk-pixbuf ->
  libtiff) needs `liblzma.pc` even for `--cflags`, and without it `meson setup`
  failed before the test suite could run.

** v0.10.7 (2026-10-25)
- Open stack popovers as panels instead of strips. A flow box's natural width is
  every entry on one line, and the popover was taking it: a folder of screenshots
  opened as a full-width strip one row tall with the grid cut off. The stack views
  now keep their own width (the flow box wraps inside it) and have a real minimum
  box -- 440x320, scrolling past 560 -- so a folder with a handful of entries
  still opens at a size worth browsing, and a long filename no longer stretches
  the grid (names ellipsize in one line, with the full name in the tooltip). The
  entries themselves are larger too: a bigger icon and a wider cell.
- Larger running-app indicators in the Mavericks themes: the dot on the glass is
  about half again as large, and so is the flat dock's CSS dot (20x7, 26x9 while
  hovered).

** v0.10.6 (2026-10-25)
- Finish the fix for the bouncing dock. v0.10.5 stopped believing the crossings
  that arrive the moment a slide stops, which slowed the bounce instead of ending
  it: a pointer parked on the edge produces a fresh crossing every time the dock
  settles. A leave is now only taken seriously when the dock is sitting still *and*
  the pointer has moved since it arrived -- a pointer that has not moved cannot
  have left -- so a stationary pointer keeps the dock out however long it rests
  there, and moving away still hides it.

** v0.10.5 (2026-10-25)
- Fix the dock bouncing at the screen edge in auto-hide mode. A pointer resting on
  the edge leaves the dock's revealing strip under it when the dock is in, and one
  pixel past the window's far edge when the dock is out, because the revealed
  window ends exactly there. The dock believed that last crossing, hid, found the
  strip under the pointer again, revealed, and went round that loop for as long as
  the pointer stayed put. Crossings that arrive while the dock is sliding -- or in
  the moment just after it stops, when they are echoes of its own movement rather
  than the pointer moving -- are now ignored, while a leave from a dock that has
  been sitting still, and the pointer has actually moved since it arrived, still
  hides it as before -- a pointer that has not moved cannot have left.

** v0.10.4 (2026-10-25)
- Draw dock separators on the glass when the dock is shaped. On a keystoned or
  tilted shelf a separator is part of the slab, not a divider standing in front of
  it: it now runs down the glass's face (it used to be confined to the icons' box,
  which is only half the slab deep) and leans with the surface, further the further
  it sits from the middle of the dock, and away from the middle as it comes
  forwards -- the way the 10.9 dock's do. The `macos-mavericks` themes give those
  separators the rod look, fading out at both ends, while a flat dock keeps the
  plain divider it had.

** v0.10.3 (2026-10-25)
- Fix the icon zoom tests so the dock can be packaged again. They build widgets,
  which a package build's sandbox has no display for, and GTK does not fail
  politely there -- it crashed. They now report the "skipped" status the other UI
  tests use, so a builder without a Wayland socket gets a clean run.

** v0.10.2 (2026-10-25)
- Make the fish-eye magnification cheap and smooth. Hovering used to ask the icon
  theme for the artwork at every pixel size the pointer passed through, and every
  one of those is a fresh lookup, decode and resample -- tens of milliseconds per
  frame, which is what made the dock feel heavy under the pointer. Icons are now
  resolved at the two sizes that matter (the resting one and the largest) and
  painted through a `GskTransform`, so magnifying costs a matrix on the GPU: a
  sweep across the whole dock went from about 40% of a core to about 6%, and the
  icons follow the pointer continuously instead of stepping from one whole pixel
  size to the next. Motion events are applied once per frame rather than once per
  event, so a high-rate mouse cannot repeat the same work before it is drawn, and
  the icons at rest are pixel-for-pixel what they were.

** v0.10.1 (2026-10-25)
- Read the XDG desktop entry cache to match windows to dock items. A compositor's
  app id and the desktop file of the application behind it rarely agree on
  spelling (`org.gnome.Nautilus` vs. a pinned `nautilus` icon, `idea` vs.
  `jetbrains-idea`, `wezterm`, `google-chrome`, ...), so the dock now indexes every
  installed entry -- the user's `~/.local/share/applications`, the Flatpak
  exports and every `$XDG_DATA_DIRS` directory -- under its desktop id, its
  `StartupWMClass`, its executable's base name and the last component of a
  reverse-DNS id, all compared case- and punctuation-insensitively. A pinned
  launcher and its running window now share one item instead of showing up twice,
  and a window whose app id needs the cache is created with the application's own
  name, icon and command. The cache is rebuilt after a minute
  (`DESKTOP_CACHE_TTL_MS`) or on demand (`lambdock_desktop_cache_invalidate()`).
- Name and icon mapping for app ids. Items are labelled with the desktop entry's
  localized `Name=` (`org.gnome.SystemMonitor` becomes "System Monitor"),
  honouring `Name[xx]` for the session's language. Applications with no desktop
  file at all -- self-built binaries, scripts, launchers -- get a derived name
  instead of their raw id: the vendor prefix is dropped, camel case and dashes are
  split into words and lower-case words are capitalised (`google-chrome` ->
  "Google Chrome", `ZFSManager` -> "ZFS Manager"). A configured icon that cannot
  be resolved from any source is replaced by the one the desktop entry names, which
  is how a preset saying `pavucontrol` gets the icon that actually ships as
  `org.pulseaudio.pavucontrol`.

** v0.10.0 (2026-10-25)
- `dock-capsule-padding`: how much room sits between the capsule's ends and the
  first/last icon, in pixels (0 … 96, default 12). It was a compile-time
  constant, which is why the dock's ends could not be matched to the OS X one;
  the theme's own button padding still adds on top of it.
- The shelf material is now measured rather than guessed: the slab is a wash that
  is densest where it meets the screen edge (~0.74 alpha) and thins out towards
  the icons (~0.38), with the 3px grey lip and the bright specular line the real
  dock has along that edge. Over a dark wallpaper it reads as glass instead of as
  a white bar, and it is defined once in the shelf modifier, so every theme that
  wears the shelf gets it.
- New theme `macos-mavericks-dark`: the same slab in smoked graphite, for dark
  sessions. Same geometry knobs, same structure, its own material.

- Fix the icon reflections. They were mirroring a `GtkWidgetPaintable` that had
  been created before its icon was ever drawn, which reports a size but paints
  nothing -- so the glass showed only the contact shadow, a flat grey strip where
  a reflection should be. The mirror now resolves the icon's artwork from the icon
  theme (icon name or gicon) and draws it at full scale, cropped by the band and
  faded as it recedes, instead of squashing the whole icon into the band. The
  contact shadow is gone with it: drawn through a linear gradient it covered the
  whole band rather than the few pixels under the icon, which is exactly the grey
  strip the reflections must not have.
- The icons now sit where the OS X dock puts them: the front glass is measured
  from the icons' bottom edge rather than counting the reflection band twice, so
  their centre lands on the slab's top edge (and a dock with reflections no longer
  floats its icons one band higher than the same dock without them).
- The Mavericks theme's running-app dot is a light glow again, like the real one,
  instead of a dark blue pill.

- The CPU, RAM and temperature meters are now *gauges* instead of text: a ring
  drawn inside one icon slot that fills with the reading and goes green → amber →
  red as it climbs, with the value written in the middle ("42%", "54°C"). The
  drawing is a real stroked arc (GskPath with round caps), it takes its text colour
  from the theme and its size from `dock-icon-size`, `ram-item`/`cpu-item`/
  `cpu-temp-item` use it by default (`#:meter? #f` brings the old text form back),
  and `dynamic-item` gained `#:meter?`, `#:unit` and `#:max` so hand-written
  gauges are three keywords away. New numeric helpers: `get-used-ram-value`,
  `get-cpu-value`, `get-cpu-temp-value` (the old string helpers still work and now
  wrap them).
- `dock-shelf-flare`: a keystone for the dock's silhouette. The edge lying on the
  screen keeps its width and the inner edge is pulled in by that many pixels per
  side, which is what makes the OS X dock's slab look like a 3D shelf instead of a
  rectangle. It is a real projection (not a shear), it works on all four edges, and
  the shelf frame reserves the room it needs so the flare is never clipped.
- The README's Mavericks recipe now carries the numbers measured off the real 10.9
  dock (a 0.92-icon slab, a 0.48-icon overhang, mirrors filling the front glass and
  a 0.54-icon flare per side), and the shelf section documents the eighth knob.
- Tests: a new `meter` C suite plus flare checks in the shelf suite (14 C suites in
  total) and 21 new Scheme assertions (184 in total).


** v0.9.0 (2026-10-25)
- Stacks: `(stack-item #:name "Downloads" #:path "~/Downloads" #:view-mode 'grid)`
  turns a dock icon into a folder popover, macOS Downloads style. The popover
  lists the folder (dot-files skipped, folders first, capped at 300 entries) in
  one of three view modes: an icon `grid`, a `list` of rows, or a `fan` of tilted
  cards painted by hand (GTK cannot rotate child widgets, so the fan snapshots
  rounded cards and hit-tests the clicks by rotating the pointer back into each
  card). The header shows the folder name, the item count and a button that opens
  the folder; the view mode can be switched from the context menu and is
  remembered per stack.
- The launchpad: `(launchpad-item)` adds an icon that opens a full screen
  layer-shell overlay with a search field and a grid of every installed
  application, parsed from the XDG desktop entries (locale-aware `Name`, `Exec`
  field codes stripped, `Hidden`/`NoDisplay` honoured, desktop ids deduplicated so
  Flatpak exports override the identical system entry). The list is rescanned on
  every open, `Escape` or a click on the backdrop closes it.
- Drag and drop: reorder icons by dragging them along the dock, drag one out of
  the dock to remove it, drop a `.desktop` file to add that application, or drop a
  folder to add it as a stack. Everything is stored in
  `$XDG_STATE_HOME/lambdock/layout-<settings>.scm` (order, removals, additions and
  per-stack view modes) and replayed on top of the settings file, so a generated
  configuration keeps working; `(dock-layout-reset! path)` forgets the overrides.
- Context menus: "Remove from Dock" on every pinned item, plus view-mode switching
  and "Open Folder" for stacks.
- New theme independent overlay stylesheet (`resources/css/overlays.css`) for
  stacks, the launchpad and the drag feedback, so they look right whatever dock
  theme is active.
- Add `dock-item-spacing` (default 4px) for the gap between dock items, plus
  `set-dock-item-spacing!` for live changes. The default reproduces the OS X
  dock's rhythm (icons about 0.3 x their size apart, counting the theme's button
  padding), and the README has a short section on spacing and sizing.
- Fix the REPL setters: `reload-dock!` was being defined in `(guile-user)`
  instead of `(lambdock core)`, so `(set-dock-theme! ...)`,
  `(set-dock-icon-size! ...)` and friends never redrew the dock.
- Tests: three new C suites (`desktop_entry`, `stack`, `dock_dnd`, 13 in total)
  and 30 new Scheme assertions (163 in total) for the new item types and the dock
  layout overrides. The C tests now run from the source root so they always
  exercise the modules in this tree.
